Water is essential to life. We need clean water for our life processes. Although the Earth's surface is mostly covered by water, only a small part of it is clean water, which we can drink and use. How then do we get enough clean water for our life processes?

Water can exist in three states of matter. It can also change from one state to another. The change of water from one state to another during the water cycle helps us obtain clean water.

Part 1 · Textbook pp. 31–32

What are the states of matter that water can exist in?

Matter can exist in different states, such as solid, liquid and gas. One example of matter that is readily available to us in all three states is water. We can find water in the solid, liquid and gaseous states around us.

🧩 Let's Connect

In the topic of Matter, you have learnt about three states of matter and their properties.

Water in the solid state is known as ice.

  • Ice can be made in the freezer at home.
  • In some countries, snow may fall during winter. Snow is made of tiny pieces of ice.
  • Large pieces of ice can be found floating in the water in very cold places, such as Antarctica. These large pieces of ice are called icebergs.

Water in the liquid state is often referred to as water.

  • Water flows from taps in our homes.
  • Rain is water in the liquid state.
  • Water can be found naturally in seas, lakes and rivers.

Water in the gaseous state is called water vapour. Water vapour can be found everywhere around us. There is water vapour in the air. Since water vapour is a colourless gas, we cannot see it.

Water vapour that is formed when water boils is also called steam. Steam is also water in the gaseous state.

How does water change from one state to another?

Water is matter. It can change from one state to another when it gains or loses heat.

🧩 Let's Connect

In the topic of Heat, you have learnt that matter can change from one state to another when it gains or loses heat.

Let us look at some examples in our daily life where water changes in state.

When ice turns into water, ice changes from the solid state to the liquid state. We call this process melting.
When water turns into steam, water changes from the liquid state to the gaseous state. That is why we can see bubbles in the water. We call this process boiling.
When water turns into ice, water changes from the liquid state to the solid state. We call this process freezing.
When steam turns into water, steam changes from the gaseous state to the liquid state. We call this process condensation.

Observation · Let's Think and Do: Analysing and interpreting data

Ice is melting, but the temperature stays the same at 0°C.

Explanation

At the start of the investigation, the temperature of ice is 0°C. Ice melts to become water at 0°C. Hence, we observe water dripping down the funnel.

The temperature of ice remains constant at 0°C until all the ice has melted.

The investigation shows that ice melts at 0°C. The temperature at which ice melts is called the melting point of ice.

🔍 Let's Explore

We often add ice cubes into a hot drink to cool it down. Can you explain why the ice cubes are no longer seen after a while?

The temperature of the ice cubes is lower than the temperature of the hot drink. Heat flows from the hot drink to the ice cubes, so the ice cubes gain heat. The heat gained causes the ice to melt at 0°C and become water, which mixes with the drink. That is why we can no longer see the ice cubes. (The drink loses heat to the ice, which is why it cools down.)

Have you ever wondered why ice melts when it is taken out of a freezer and placed on a table? Let's explain this.

Observation:

Ice melts when it is taken out of a freezer and placed on a table.

What you have learnt:

Heat flows from a place of higher temperature to a place of lower temperature.

Explanation for the melting of ice:

The temperature of ice is lower than the temperature of the surroundings. Therefore, ice will gain heat from its surroundings. The heat gained by ice causes it to melt at 0°C.
🌍 Science Around Us

Animals such as polar bears need ice caps to survive. However, due to increasing human activities such as the increased use of transportation and electronic devices, more fossil fuels are burnt to produce more energy. With more fossil fuels burnt, more greenhouse gases are produced. Greenhouse gases trap heat and cause a rise in the Earth's temperature. This causes the ice in the Arctic and Antarctica to melt faster. With fewer ice caps due to melting, the polar bears' survival is threatened.

We can do our part to slow down the rise in the Earth's temperature. For example, we can take public transport and turn off electronic devices when we are not using them.

Condensation

If you place a cool pot cover over a pot of boiling water, you will notice water droplets forming on the underside of the pot cover. Where do the water droplets come from?

1Bubbles that are produced when water boils contain steam.

Have you ever wondered why water droplets appear on the cold surface of a can of drink that is taken out of the refrigerator? This is because condensation can also happen when water vapour in the air is in contact with a cold surface.

When water vapour in the air comes in contact with the cold surface of the can, the water vapour loses heat to the cold surface. When water vapour loses heat, it changes into water droplets. Water vapour changes from the gaseous state to the liquid state.

🔍 Let's Explore

You will notice mist near the mouth of a kettle when water boils. Think about the heat loss when steam comes in contact with the air outside the kettle. What do you think the mist is made of?

The mist is made of tiny water droplets (water in the liquid state). The steam coming out of the kettle is hotter than the air outside, so it loses heat to the cooler air and condenses into tiny water droplets that we can see as mist. Right at the mouth of the spout there is a small clear gap: that is steam, which is colourless and cannot be seen.

Part 7 · Textbook pp. 46–47

Evaporation

Have you observed how wet clothes left out under the Sun become dry after a couple of hours? Or how puddles of water left behind after a heavy rain are not seen after a few hours? These happen because water changes into water vapour during evaporation.

Evaporation involves a change in state from liquid to gas, similar to boiling. However, evaporation can take place at any temperature, unlike boiling. Boiling of water takes place only at 100°C.

What factors affect how quickly evaporation takes place?

Wind, temperature and exposed surface area can affect how quickly evaporation takes place.

💨 Wind

Wind affects how quickly water evaporates. The stronger the wind, the more quickly water evaporates. That is why wet clothes dry more quickly on a windy day.

☀️ Temperature

Temperature affects how quickly water evaporates. Water evaporates more quickly if the temperature of the surroundings is higher. That is why wet clothes dry more quickly when hung out on a hotter day.

👕 Exposed surface area

Exposed surface area affects how quickly water evaporates. The larger the exposed surface area, the more quickly water evaporates. That is why wet clothes that are spread out dry more quickly than wet clothes that are stacked together.

The water cycle is very important to our daily life. It helps to provide all living things with a continuous supply of water for survival.

Humans need clean water to survive. While water covers most of the Earth's surface, very little of it is clean water.

During the water cycle, water in water bodies evaporates to form water vapour, leaving behind impurities such as dirt. The water vapour condenses to form tiny water droplets, which gather to form clouds. When rain falls from the clouds, rainwater is collected in reservoirs and has to be treated before it is safe for drinking.

🌍 Science Around Us

Even though Singapore is surrounded by seas, we cannot use the seawater directly for our daily needs. Instead, we need to remove the salt and impurities in the seawater before we can use the water for activities such as drinking, cooking and watering plants. Singapore has desalination plants to produce clean water from seawater. These desalination plants ensure that we have clean water for our daily needs.

Key idea
The continuous processes of evaporation and condensation during the water cycle ensure that there is a continuous supply of water.

Part 10 · Textbook pp. 51–54

What is the impact of water pollution?

Human activity is the most common reason for water pollution. Water pollution causes water in the environment to become unsafe for use.

Rubbish thrown by humans into water bodies causes water pollution.

Oil from ships and boats can spill into the sea, causing the water to be polluted.

Water pollution can damage our environment and harm living things.

Water pollution can cause excessive growth and spread of algae in lakes. These algae then compete with the other living things in the water for oxygen. When the other living things do not have sufficient oxygen, they will die.

Water pollution harms sea creatures. Chemicals and trash thrown into the water may poison and kill the sea creatures.

When we eat fish from polluted water bodies and when we use the polluted water for activities such as farming, harmful substances can be introduced into our bodies.
