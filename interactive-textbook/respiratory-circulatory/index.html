    When we breathe in, air enters our body through our nose. The air that is breathed in is called inhaled air.

    As air passes through the nose, dust is trapped by the tiny hairs in the nose. This filtered air is also warmed and moistened.

    From the nose, the air enters the windpipe.

    From the windpipe, the air enters the lungs and fills the air sacs. Gaseous exchange happens in the lungs.

    Gaseous exchange occurs when oxygen in air enters the blood in the lungs, and carbon dioxide is removed from the blood.

    During gaseous exchange, all the gases in air enter the air sacs at the same time. It is not only oxygen that enters the air sacs and it is not only carbon dioxide that leaves the air sacs.

    Oxygen moves from the air sacs into the blood, while carbon dioxide moves from the blood to the air sacs.

    The human circulatory system

    Look at the diagram of the human circulatory system above. You can see that blood vessels are found all over the body. These blood vessels are like rail tracks linking different parts of the body so that substances can be transported. Blood flows inside the blood vessels like trains moving on tracks.     💡 I'm Inspired

    The first stethoscope was invented by a doctor, René Théophile Hyacinthe Laënnec, in 1816. It was a short, wooden tube with an earpiece and a hole. Laënnec often used it to listen to the various sounds made by the lungs and the hearts of his patients.

    The first stethoscope · Laënnec using the first stethoscope on a patient

    At first, many doctors did not appreciate the use of the stethoscope. They felt that it was best to listen to sounds made by the lungs and the heart using their own ears instead. However, that did not stop Laënnec. He continued to use it to diagnose diseases and diligently recorded his observations. He then shared his methods and findings, many of which are still relevant in the field of medicine today.

    The design of stethoscopes has continued to be improved over the years. This has allowed medical professionals such as doctors to listen to the sounds of the heart, the lungs and the flow of blood more precisely.

    🧩 Let's Connect

    In the topic of Human Systems, you have learnt about parts and functions of the muscular, skeletal and digestive systems.

    Before a vigorous activity such as a run, Devi's and Chen's heart rates were at the resting level. The resting level refers to the heart beating at a slower pace when they are less active, such as when they are sitting or lying in bed.

    When resting, the respiratory system and the circulatory system work together to ensure that substances such as oxygen and digested food are transported to different parts of the body.

    After their run, the breathing rates and heart rates of Devi and Chen increased.

    Before a run

    DeviMy heart rate is 72 beats per minute.

    After a run

    DeviMy heart rate is 98 beats per minute now!

    ChenWow, it has increased! How about your breathing rate?

    When Devi and Chen run, more oxygen and digested food need to be delivered to their muscles to enable them to run. Besides the muscular and skeletal systems, the digestive, respiratory and circulatory systems also need to work together.

    The skeletal system and the muscular system help Devi and Chen move. When muscles work, more carbon dioxide is produced and this needs to be removed at a faster rate as well.
    To take in more oxygen and give out more carbon dioxide from the body, Devi and Chen breathe faster. This increases their breathing rates.
    To transport more oxygen from the lungs to the muscles and more carbon dioxide from the muscles to the lungs, the heart needs to pump faster. The heart also pumps faster to provide more digested food for energy. As such, Devi's and Chen's heart rates also increase.
    The digestive system also needs to work faster to digest food so that it can also be transported to the muscles.

    🌏 Science Around Us

    It is important for us to take good care of our respiratory and circulatory systems. One way is adopting healthy habits! This means eating right, maintaining an active lifestyle and avoiding harmful activities.

    A balanced diet

    Eating right reduces the chances of having harmful fatty deposits along our blood vessels. This allows for more blood to be carried by the blood vessels and for our heart to pump blood more efficiently.

    An active lifestyle, including regular exercise such as jogging and swimming, can also strengthen our heart and prevent heart diseases.

    Children exercising

    Another way to ensure that our lungs are in good condition is to avoid harmful activities such as smoking. Such activities introduce harmful chemicals into our body and can cause illnesses.
